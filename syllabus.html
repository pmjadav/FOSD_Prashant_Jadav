Course & Syllabus

Fundamentals of Operating System Design · CEUC301

Course focus: Analyze operating-system mechanisms and evaluate design choices using performance, reliability, security, scalability and workload characteristics.

Course Outcomes

CO1

Analyze OS design goals and justify architectural choices such as monolithic, microkernel and hybrid architectures.

CO2

Analyze processes, threads and scheduling algorithms using throughput, turnaround, waiting, response and fairness metrics.

CO3

Design and evaluate synchronization and concurrency-control mechanisms using semaphores, monitors and lock-free techniques.

CO4

Analyze deadlocks and design prevention, avoidance, detection and recovery strategies.

CO5

Assess memory-management and virtual-memory designs using fragmentation, latency, security and scalability trade-offs.

CO6

Evaluate file-system and I/O subsystem designs including scheduling, buffering, journaling and log-structured approaches.

CO7

Synthesize OS design principles across real-time, embedded, mobile and other specialized domains.

Syllabus Overview

UnitMajor Topics
1OS introduction, design goals, types, kernel architectures, process image, PCB, system calls, user/kernel mode.
2Processes, process states, context switching, threads, TCB, process vs. thread.
3Scheduling metrics, workload characterization, FCFS, SJF, SRTF, RR, priority, multilevel queue/feedback, EDF, RMS.
4Synchronization, concurrency, semaphores, monitors, deadlock models, prevention, avoidance, detection and recovery.
5Relocation, protection, sharing, fixed/dynamic partitioning, fragmentation, compaction, first/best/worst/next fit, paging, segmentation, TLB.
6File-system concepts, allocation, free-space management, directory structures, disk scheduling, buffering and journaling.
7Real-time, embedded, mobile and distributed operating-system design principles and case studies.

Recommended Books

  • Operating System Concepts — Abraham Silberschatz, Peter B. Galvin, Greg Gagne
  • Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces — Remzi H. Arpaci-Dusseau and Andrea C. Arpaci-Dusseau
  • Modern Operating Systems — Andrew S. Tanenbaum and Herbert Bos
  • Operating Systems: Internals and Design Principles — William Stallings
